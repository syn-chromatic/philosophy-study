## **Thales:** 

**First Greek Philosopher (\~624-546 BC)**



Lived in Miletus, a coastal city on the western coast of Anatolia (present-day Turkey), in the region of Ionia. Ionia was home to Greek settlements and served as an important shipping route connecting the Greek world with Anatolia.



Thales was the first of many things, he was the first philosopher, western astronomer, scientist, and Greek mathematician.



He does not have any record of his books, but he was first to predict a solar eclipse, and this can be traced down to an exact date of May 28th, 585 BC.



His main contribution to Philosophy was his search for the Arche that asked what is the universe made of? the stuff that connects everything, the stuff the universe is made of.



The arche had to explain these things:

* It had to explain existence, and the ground for existence itself. 
* It had to explain change, how things grow and shrink, and what is the cause of that.
* It had to explain motion, how things change from one place to another
* It had to explain life



His conclusion for what everything is made of is Water, because it explains existence as the substance exists in three states, as a solid, liquid, or gas, and it explains change, because it changes its own form to other states, as it can melt, freeze, condense, and evaporate on any surface, all by itself, and it explains motion, because it also moves all by itself, as he observed the ocean and how it moves all on its own, no one is pushing the waves, and it explains life because all living things depend on it to survive, because without water life does not exist.





## **Anaximander:**

**Second Greek Philosopher (\~610-546 BC)**



Was born probably a few years after Thales, but died at about the same time, like Thales he lived in Miletus and was seen as Thales's student.



Anaximander took science into a whole new level, it is said he went to Sparta to set up a Gnomon, this is a simple pillar that is set up straight with markings on the ground, and when the sun shines you can where and how long the shadow is, that way you can do measurements for time and calendar dates.



He was the first to attempt a map of the entire world.



His theories:

* He believed that the earth did not float on water like Thales did
* He believed that the earth is floating in empty space, because he did not believe that there is absolute up or down
* He disagreed with Thales that the world was a flat disk
* He believed that the earth was a cylinder with 3:1 ratio of its dimensions
* He believed that the sun and the stars were not objects but were rings of fire covered in smoke, and that the sun was just part of the ring peeking from the ring of fire



He is seen as the "Father of Evolution" because of his theory of evolution, since he believed that all life must have changed over time, and that the first type of animals must have been fish or fish-like and water-based, and that humans must have descended from some kind of animal.



For example his reasoning for speculating about the animal origin of humans is because humans nurture their children for several years while most animal young are independent much quicker, so he believed that humans used to live and grow inside animals until puberty because there's no other way they could have survived.



And for the origin of the first animals, they were formed in the sea or some kind of warm water, but they weren't living at normal and free life, instead they lived inside a cocoon-like structure, that was made out of thorny bark, and they kept living inside those cocoons until they migrated to land and broke free.



Anaximander added another theory for Thales's theory of life, by believing that everything had opposites, for example the opposites of heat and cold, or the opposites of wet and dry, and if they combine they cancel each other out.



In modern day, we would refer to this as exchange of energy, but in Anaximander's time, he considers it as an obligatory penalty, rather than a voluntary one, as opposites must pay their penalty eventually, he even took it one step further by saying that the hot summer had to pay a fine to the moderate temperature, because of an injustice that was committed against it, and similarly with cold winter.



He believed that opposites have separated out from the original Arche/Substance, and that all opposites are infinite because they do not have a material substance, so they are not finite in resource, and he called this infinite substance Apeiron, and he referred to it as The Divine.

#### **Summary of all three Greek Philosophers (The Milesians):**

* ###### **Thales:** Formulated the primary question of cosmology by searching for a single physical material (water) to account for the physical state, motion, and origin of all observable phenomena.
* ###### **Anaximander:** Critiqued Thales' reliance on a specific material element, arguing that a single finite substance could not yield its opposite (e.g., water generating fire). He proposed the abstract, boundless Apeiron, introducing principles of cosmic equilibrium and biological transmutation.
* ###### **Anaximenes:** Synthesized both positions by retaining Anaximander's boundless, dynamic qualities while grounding the arche in a observable substance (air). By introducing rarefaction and condensation, he offered the first formal mechanism explaining how quantitative changes in density yield qualitative material transformations.
